# Evidence-Aware Academic Writing

Start by identifying the target audience, venue or format constraints, central
claim, supplied evidence, and the requested deliverable. Preserve technical
meaning while improving structure, precision, and readability.

Use a claim-evidence distinction throughout: material supplied by the user or
checked in the workspace may be summarized as evidence; proposed wording,
interpretation, and missing information must be labeled accordingly. Do not
create bibliographic entries or report numerical findings unless their source is
available in the task context.

For reviews, prioritize correctness, reproducibility, clarity, limitations, and
actionable revision steps. Feedback should quote or point to the relevant
passage when possible and avoid representing a recommendation as a verified
fact.
